dB Meter

Decibel meter and noise dose for iPhone and iPad

See how loud it is around you, live, in decibels. The full sound meter is free forever: no ads, no paywall at launch, no weekly plans.

dB Meter on the App Store

What you see

  • A large live reading in dBA (A-weighted, Fast response), with min, average and max as you measure. The average is the true sound energy average (LAeq).
  • One scale in five zones, from Quiet to Dangerous, with the 85 dBA 8-hour limit marked, an everyday comparison and a graph of the last minute.
  • “<30” when it is quieter than the microphone can hear, and OVER when the input overloads.
  • A summary when you stop: average, min, max and peak, to share as an image.
  • Every measurement of a few seconds or more is saved, with date, time and length. Search by level, a dB value or a date.
  • It keeps measuring with the screen locked, for up to 8 hours. On iPhone a Live Activity shows the level on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island, with Stop.

Today's noise dose

dB Meter adds up how much of today's daily limit you have used, counted like a NIOSH dosimeter: 85 dBA for 8 hours is 100%, every 3 dB louder halves the time, and sound below 80 dBA adds nothing. It counts the time you measure, also with the screen locked, not the rest of your day.

Widgets, Siri and Control Center

  • Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets show your latest measurement and today's dose. A tap starts measuring.
  • Ask Siri “How loud is it with dB Meter?” and hear the level after a few seconds. Shortcuts can also tell you today's noise dose.
  • Start measuring from Control Center or the Action button (iOS 18 and later).

An honest estimate

Readings come from your phone's microphone, so they are estimates. dB Meter is not a medical device or a certified sound level meter, and not a tool for workplace compliance or legal use. If you have a calibrated meter, you can match dB Meter to it in Settings, for free. How accurate is it?
